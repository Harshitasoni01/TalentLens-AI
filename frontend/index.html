Why screening breaks down

Hiring teams hit the same three walls, and a keyword filter fixes none of them.

Volume

A single backend role can attract 1,000+ applicants. Nobody reads them all carefully.

Inconsistency

Two recruiters can read the same resume and reach two different verdicts.

Keyword bias

A tutorial project and production work look identical when you only count keywords.

How it works

From job post to shortlist in four steps

  1. 1

    Describe the role

    Title, skills, experience range, location. This is what every candidate is measured against.

  2. 2

    Upload resumes

    Drop in PDF or DOCX files, one or many.

  3. 3

    AI reads and scores

    Each resume becomes a structured profile, then gets scored against the role.

  4. 4

    Review the shortlist

    Open a ranked list with reasons and missing skills for every candidate.

Features

Built for the way recruiters actually work

Resume understanding

Skills, experience, projects, education and companies are pulled out of raw resume text.

Reasoned scoring

Every score from 0 to 100 comes with plain-language reasons you can read and question.

Skill-gap view

See exactly which required skills a candidate does not show, before the interview.

Retry without losing work

If the AI hits a limit mid-batch, finished candidates stay saved and the rest can be retried.

Tier filters

Switch between strong, moderate and weak matches, and search by name or skill.

One workspace per role

Every job keeps its own candidates, scores and processing status.

Stop reading resumes top to bottom.

Start at the strongest candidate instead.
